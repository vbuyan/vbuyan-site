The node shares its disk with production work, and that disk normally sits just above 5% free. The
Kubernetes default disk eviction threshold is... 5% free. On a big disk that is far more space than the
pods themselves will ever need.

![Free disk space vs the default eviction threshold](/img/eviction-storm.png)

One afternoon the line dipped under it. The kubelet marked the node with DiskPressure and evicted
everything. Controllers recreated the pods, the kubelet evicted them again. Three waves over five and a
half hours.

The worst part: Prometheus was one of the evicted pods. The graph has a hole exactly where the incident
is. I found it by accident, while working on something else.

Fixes:

1. An absolute eviction floor instead of a percentage. Percentage defaults are written with small disks in mind.
2. Two new alerts: node pressure conditions and evicted pods.
3. A written postmortem, with the timeline rebuilt from what Prometheus did manage to keep.

Lesson: if you only alert on problems you have already seen, the new ones stay invisible. And monitoring
that runs on the node it watches can go down with it.
